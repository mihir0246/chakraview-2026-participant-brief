00 // The Council's Mandate

What is CHAKRAVYUH?

CHAKRAVYUH is a month-long, high-intensity executive simulation engineered to test, develop, and elevate your leadership competency. The initiative creates a parallel operating system within the club. As a participant, you will not just be attending events—you will be planning, funding, executing, and defending them.

The Idea Behind the Name

Element Meaning in Chakravyuh
Chakra A connected wheel: every part contributes to the movement of the whole.
Vyuh A strategic multi-layered formation: navigating operational challenges, execution, and defense.
Council Leadership is treated as a collective system of responsibilities, not a one-person hierarchy.
Office Each participant is strictly accountable for one portfolio while remaining responsible for the success of the full council.

01 // The Ten Sabhasads

Active Rotaractors will be divided into balanced Councils of exactly 10 members each. Within your Council, there are no hierarchical titles. Every member holds a specific "Sabhasad" office with equal responsibility but differentiated accountability.

Sabhasad — Nayak

The Visionary: The chief representative of the Council. Responsible for setting the strategic roadmap (PoA), aligning the team's agenda, charting the vision, and ensuring overall direction and momentum.

Sabhasad — Lekhak

The Scribe: The master of records and governance. Tasked with meticulously documenting all proceedings, drafting the formal Minutes of Meetings, and compiling compliance dossiers and project reports.

Sabhasad — Senapati

The Commander: The operations chief. Drives internal synergy, manages the delegation of tasks on the ground, and actively resolves any crises, contingencies, or roadblocks that arise.

Sabhasad — Bhandari

The Treasurer: The guardian of the treasury. Manages the council's budget, conceptualizes and executes micro-fundraisers, and ensures absolute financial transparency through audited ledgers.

Sabhasad — Saarthi

The Guide: The driver of people and engagement. Focuses on maintaining high attendance, resolving internal conflicts, building team morale, and empowering other members with active roles.

Sabhasad — Pahredar

The Guardian: The keeper of order and protocol. Ensures meeting decorum, strict punctuality, quorum fulfillment, and conducts thorough venue and technical readiness checks before events.

Sabhasad — Karta (x4)

The Execution Force: The core operators of the Council. These 4 unified offices share the responsibility of bringing the assigned Avenue deliverables to life, handling everything from physical ground initiatives to digital requirements.

02 // The Sabha Draft (The Council Auction)

How are the 10-member Councils formed? They are not randomly assigned. They are built through strategy, valuation, and commerce in a high-stakes draft.

The Draft Mechanics

1. The Call for Leaders: All interested participants must fill out the official CHAKRAVYUH Entry Google Form. The Executive Core will evaluate applications based on operational readiness and strategic vision to handpick the individuals who will act as the Nayaks (Council Leaders).

2. The Virtual Treasury: Each selected Nayak will be granted a fixed virtual purse to build their council from the ground up.

3. The Player Pool: The remaining participants will enter the open auction pool.

4. The Live Bidding War: In a live, closed-room auction, the Nayaks will bid against each other to draft their remaining 9 team members. Nayaks must carefully manage their treasury to secure a fully capable 10-member Council!

04 // Domains of Execution

Upon formation, each Council will be assigned two distinct avenues. These two avenues become your Council's principal project battlefield for the month. You will be required to execute flagship events, digital campaigns, and community interventions based entirely on these assigned domains.

What to Expect

Depending on your Council's allocation, your team might be tasked with executing high-impact beneficiary donation drives, hosting massive external speaker sessions, organizing campus-wide sports tournaments, pulling off physical flash mobs, securing corporate sponsorships, or drafting international diplomatic agreements.

In addition, all Councils are required to maintain a universal communications mandate—including high-quality reveal reels, event teasers, and synchronized PR broadcasts across the campus.

05 // Maryada & The Raj Sabha

Maryada: The Code of Conduct

Your Council will be evaluated by an internal scoring system tracking your efficiency, reach, and execution. However, strict adherence to the rules is mandatory. Severe consequences will be applied to your Council's standing for:

  • Late reporting or missed deadlines.
  • Financial discrepancies, missing bills, or unaccounted funds.
  • Failure to maintain decorum or punctuality during simulation meetings.
  • Academic Mauna Violations: Conducting any physical execution events between Oct 12-17 (beyond permitted meetings and promotions) will result in immediate disqualification of the event.

Raj Sabha: The Final Council

On 28 October 2026, the simulation culminates in the Raj Sabha. Each Council must present and defend its month-long tenure before a Jury Panel composed of the actual club Executive Core.

Stage Duration Protocol & Requirements
Executive Pitch 15 Minutes (Uninterrupted) Present operational delivery across both avenues, reach metrics, financial statements, and Council dynamics.
Cross-Examination 10 Minutes Formal questioning by the Jury. The Nayak, Lekhak, and Bhandari must be prepared to defend their records.

06 // The Council Charter

  • Own the Office: Your Sabhasad belongs to you. Track its deadlines, execute its duties, and own the outcomes.
  • Serve the Council: A successful individual portfolio does not compensate for a failing team. Help your fellow Sabhasads.
  • Document the Work: No claim should depend on memory alone. Preserve your evidence, photos, and bills.
  • Account for Every Rupee: Financial transparency is a strict responsibility, not an afterthought.
  • Cross the Walls: Support other Councils. Attend their events. True leadership is collaborative.
  • Defend the Chronicle: Your Council must be able to explain what it promised, what it delivered, what changed, and why.
